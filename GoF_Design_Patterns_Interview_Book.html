Part 1 · Foundations

GoF Fundamentals

WHAT ARE THE GoF PATTERNS?

  • Published in 1994: “Design Patterns: Elements of Reusable Object-Oriented Software” by Erich Gamma, Richard Helm, Ralph Johnson and John Vlissides.
  • 23 patterns, in 3 categories: Creational (5), Structural (7), Behavioral (11).
  • A pattern is a named, proven, reusable solution to a recurring design problem in a given context. It is a template, not finished code.

ELEMENTS OF EVERY PATTERN

  • Name, Problem (intent and context), Solution (participants and collaborations), Consequences (trade-offs).

Q1. What is a design pattern?

A design pattern is a general, reusable solution to a commonly occurring problem in software design. It is a description or template for solving the problem, which you adapt to your context, rather than a finished piece of code or library.

Q2. Who are the “Gang of Four” and what is their book?

Erich Gamma, Richard Helm, Ralph Johnson and John Vlissides. Their 1994 book “Design Patterns: Elements of Reusable Object-Oriented Software” catalogued 23 object-oriented patterns and gave developers a shared vocabulary.

↑ Contents7

GoF Fundamentals · continued

Q3. How many GoF patterns are there and how are they categorized?

There are 23, grouped by purpose: 5 Creational (object creation), 7 Structural (composition of classes and objects) and 11 Behavioral (communication and responsibility between objects).

Q4. What is the purpose of Creational patterns? Name them.

They abstract the instantiation process so a system is independent of how its objects are created, composed and represented. They are Singleton, Factory Method, Abstract Factory, Builder and Prototype.

Q5. What is the purpose of Structural patterns? Name them.

They explain how to assemble classes and objects into larger structures while keeping those structures flexible and efficient. They are Adapter, Bridge, Composite, Decorator, Facade, Flyweight and Proxy.

Q6. What is the purpose of Behavioral patterns? Name them.

They deal with algorithms and the assignment of responsibilities between objects. They are Chain of Responsibility, Command, Interpreter, Iterator, Mediator, Memento, Observer, State, Strategy, Template Method and Visitor.

↑ Contents8

GoF Fundamentals · continued

Q7. What is the difference between class patterns and object patterns?

Class patterns deal with relationships between classes through inheritance and are fixed at compile time (Factory Method, Adapter (class), Template Method, Interpreter). Object patterns deal with relationships between objects through composition and can change at runtime (most others).

Q8. What are the essential elements of a design pattern?

Four elements: the pattern name (a shared vocabulary), the problem (when to apply it), the solution (the participants, their relationships and collaborations) and the consequences (the trade-offs and results of applying it).

Q9. What are the benefits of using design patterns?

They provide proven solutions, a common vocabulary for teams, better reuse and maintainability, loosely coupled designs that are easier to extend, and faster design discussions. They also help in understanding frameworks such as Spring and the JDK.

Q10. What are the drawbacks or criticisms of design patterns?

Overuse leads to needless complexity (“pattern-itis”), indirection and more classes. Some patterns compensate for language limitations and become trivial with lambdas or other features. Applying a pattern without a real problem is over-engineering.

↑ Contents9

GoF Fundamentals · continued

Q11. How is a design pattern different from an algorithm, a framework and an idiom?

An algorithm is a precise sequence of steps; a pattern is a higher-level structural blueprint. A framework is concrete reusable code that calls your code; a pattern is an abstract idea. An idiom is a language-specific low-level technique (such as try-with-resources), whereas patterns are language-independent.

Q12. What is an anti-pattern? Give examples.

An anti-pattern is a commonly used solution that looks helpful but produces more problems than it solves. Examples: God Object, Spaghetti Code, Golden Hammer, Copy-and-Paste Programming, Singleton abuse, Lava Flow and Magic Numbers.

Principles, UML and Pattern Selection

CORE IDEAS BEHIND THE PATTERNS

  • Program to an interface, not an implementation.
  • Favor object composition over class inheritance.
  • Encapsulate what varies; depend on abstractions; loose coupling, high cohesion.

UML RELATIONSHIPS

  • Dependency (uses), Association (knows), Aggregation (has-a, shared lifetime), Composition (owns, same lifetime), Generalization (is-a, extends), Realization (implements).

Q13. What are the two main design principles stated by the GoF?

“Program to an interface, not an implementation” and “Favor object composition over class inheritance.” Most of the 23 patterns are applications of these two ideas.

Q14. Why should we favor composition over inheritance?

Inheritance is static, breaks encapsulation (subclass depends on parent internals) and tightly couples classes. Composition is dynamic, lets behaviour be swapped at runtime, supports small focused classes and avoids deep hierarchies. Strategy, Decorator, Bridge and State all rely on it.

↑ Contents12

Principles, UML and Pattern Selection · continued

Q15. How do the SOLID principles relate to design patterns?

Patterns are concrete applications of SOLID: Strategy, Decorator and Visitor support Open/Closed; Adapter and Facade support Interface Segregation and Dependency Inversion; Factory and Abstract Factory support Dependency Inversion; each pattern tends to isolate one responsibility (SRP).

Q16. Which patterns best support the Open/Closed Principle?

Strategy, Decorator, Template Method, Observer, Factory Method, Command and Visitor (for new operations). New behaviour is added by writing new classes instead of modifying existing, tested ones.

Q17. Explain the main UML relationships used in pattern diagrams.

Dependency is a temporary “uses”; association is a long-term “knows”; aggregation is a whole-part where the part can exist alone; composition is a whole-part where the part dies with the whole; generalization is inheritance; realization is a class implementing an interface.

↑ Contents13

Principles, UML and Pattern Selection · continued

Q18. How do you choose the right design pattern for a problem?

Identify what varies and what must stay stable, state the problem in the pattern’s terms (for example, “creation logic changes” or “algorithm should be swappable”), review the intent and consequences of candidate patterns, check for simpler solutions first, and keep the design only if it reduces change cost.

Q19. Which GoF patterns are used in the Java core library (JDK)?

Factory Method (Calendar.getInstance), Builder (StringBuilder), Singleton (Runtime), Prototype (Object.clone), Adapter (InputStreamReader), Decorator (BufferedInputStream), Proxy (java.lang.reflect.Proxy), Iterator, Observer (listeners), Strategy (Comparator), Template Method (AbstractList) and Flyweight (Integer.valueOf).

Q20. Which GoF patterns are used in the Spring Framework?

Singleton (default bean scope), Factory (BeanFactory, ApplicationContext), Proxy (AOP, @Transactional), Template Method (JdbcTemplate, RestTemplate), Observer (ApplicationEvent), Strategy (Resource, TransactionManager), Decorator (BeanDefinitionDecorator, wrappers), Adapter (HandlerAdapter) and Front Controller (DispatcherServlet).

Part 2 · Creational Patterns

Singleton

INTENT

  • Ensure a class has only one instance and provide a global point of access to it.

PROBLEM AND SOLUTION

  • Need exactly one object (configuration, logger, cache, thread pool, registry).
  • Make the constructor private, keep a private static instance, expose a public static accessor.

IN THE JDK

  • java.lang.Runtime.getRuntime(), java.awt.Desktop.getDesktop().
public enum AppConfig {          // thread-safe, serialization-safe
    INSTANCE;
    public String get(String key) { /* ... */ return null; }
}
// Holder idiom (lazy, thread-safe, no locking)
public final class Registry {
    private Registry() {}
    private static class Holder { static final Registry I = new Registry(); }
    public static Registry getInstance() { return Holder.I; }
}
↑ Contents16

Singleton · continued

Q21. What is the Singleton pattern and when do you use it?

Singleton guarantees a single instance of a class and a global access point. Use it when exactly one object must coordinate actions across the system, such as a configuration manager, connection pool, cache or logger.

Q22. How do you implement a thread-safe Singleton in Java?

Options: eager static initialization; a synchronized getInstance (simple but slow); double-checked locking with a volatile field; the Bill Pugh holder idiom; or a single-element enum. The holder idiom and the enum are usually preferred.

Q23. What is the difference between eager and lazy initialization?

Eager creates the instance at class-load time: simple and thread-safe, but wastes resources if never used. Lazy creates it on first request: saves resources but needs care for thread safety.

Q24. What is double-checked locking and why does the field need volatile?

It checks for null, enters a synchronized block, and checks again before creating the object, avoiding locking on every call. Without volatile, instruction reordering may publish a reference to a partially constructed object to another thread; volatile (Java 5+) prevents this.

↑ Contents17

Singleton · continued

Q25. What is the Bill Pugh (initialization-on-demand holder) Singleton?

A private static inner class holds the instance as a static final field. The JVM loads the holder only when getInstance() is first called, and class initialization is guaranteed thread-safe, so you get lazy loading without explicit synchronization.

Q26. Why is an enum considered the best way to implement a Singleton?

As Joshua Bloch recommends, an enum with one constant is thread-safe, handles serialization automatically, and is immune to reflection attacks because the JVM forbids instantiating enums. Its limits are that it cannot extend another class and it cannot be lazily initialized with parameters.

Q27. How can a Singleton be broken and how do you protect it?

Reflection (calling the private constructor), serialization/deserialization (creates a new object), clone(), and multiple classloaders. Defend by throwing from the constructor if an instance exists, implementing readResolve(), overriding clone() to throw, or using an enum.

↑ Contents18

Singleton · continued

Q28. What are the disadvantages of Singleton? Why is it called an anti-pattern?

It introduces hidden global state, hides dependencies, makes unit testing and mocking hard, violates the Single Responsibility Principle (it manages its own lifecycle), and complicates concurrent and distributed environments.

Q29. What is the difference between a Singleton and a static class?

A Singleton is a real object: it can implement interfaces, be passed around, be lazily initialized, and be mocked or extended. A static class has only static members, cannot implement an interface polymorphically and is loaded eagerly.

Q30. Is a Spring singleton bean the same as the GoF Singleton?

No. A Spring singleton is one instance per ApplicationContext (container), managed by the container, whereas the GoF Singleton is one instance per classloader enforced by the class itself. Spring beans can be injected and mocked, which avoids the testing problems of the GoF version.

Factory Method

INTENT

  • Define an interface for creating an object, but let subclasses decide which class to instantiate.

PROBLEM AND SOLUTION

  • A class cannot anticipate the class of objects it must create.
  • Replace direct new with a creator method that subclasses override.

IN THE JDK

  • Calendar.getInstance(), NumberFormat.getInstance(), Integer.valueOf(), List.of(), Collection.iterator(), Charset.forName().

Q31. What is the Factory Method pattern?

It defines a method for creating an object in a superclass and lets subclasses override it to decide which concrete product to create. It defers instantiation to subclasses and removes the dependency on concrete classes from the client.

Q32. What is the difference between a Simple Factory and the Factory Method pattern?

A simple (static) factory is one class with a method that uses a conditional to choose the product; it is an idiom, not a GoF pattern. Factory Method uses inheritance and polymorphism: each subclass of the creator overrides the factory method, so new products need no modification of existing code.

↑ Contents20

Factory Method · continued

Q33. What are the participants of Factory Method?

Product (interface), ConcreteProduct, Creator (declares the factory method and often uses it in other operations), and ConcreteCreator (overrides the factory method to return a ConcreteProduct).

Q34. When should you use Factory Method?

When a class cannot know in advance which objects it must create, when you want subclasses to specify the objects, when you want to localize creation logic, or when you want to give library or framework users a way to extend internal components.

Q35. What are the benefits of Factory Method?

Loose coupling between creator and concrete products, Single Responsibility (creation code in one place), Open/Closed (add products without changing clients), and easy substitution of products in tests.

Q36. Give examples of Factory Method in the JDK.

Calendar.getInstance(), NumberFormat.getCurrencyInstance(), Integer.valueOf(), Collection.iterator() (each collection returns its own Iterator), URLStreamHandlerFactory and Class.forName()-based factories.

↑ Contents21

Factory Method · continued

Q37. How does Factory Method differ from Abstract Factory?

Factory Method is a single method, uses inheritance, and creates one product. Abstract Factory is an object, uses composition, and creates a family of related products. Abstract Factories are often implemented with several factory methods.

Q38. What is a parameterized factory method?

A factory method that takes an argument (for example a type or key) and decides which product to return. It reduces the number of creator subclasses but reintroduces conditional logic, so combine it with a registry map to stay open for extension.

Q39. What are the drawbacks of Factory Method?

It can lead to many parallel creator subclasses, one per product, and adds a layer of indirection. For simple creation, a plain constructor or a static factory is easier to read.

Abstract Factory

INTENT

  • Provide an interface for creating families of related or dependent objects without specifying their concrete classes.

PROBLEM AND SOLUTION

  • Products must be used together (for example Windows buttons with Windows checkboxes).
  • A factory interface declares one creation method per product; each concrete factory returns one consistent family.

IN THE JDK

  • javax.xml.parsers.DocumentBuilderFactory, TransformerFactory, XPathFactory, java.sql.Connection (creates Statement and PreparedStatement families).

Q40. What is the Abstract Factory pattern?

It provides an interface for creating families of related objects without naming their concrete classes. The client works only with abstract factories and abstract products, so switching the whole family is a one-line change.

Q41. What are the participants of Abstract Factory?

AbstractFactory (declares creation methods), ConcreteFactory (implements them for one family), AbstractProduct (one interface per product type), ConcreteProduct (family-specific), and Client (uses only the abstract types).

↑ Contents24

Abstract Factory · continued

Q42. Give a real-world example of Abstract Factory.

A cross-platform UI toolkit: GUIFactory with createButton() and createCheckbox(), implemented by WindowsFactory and MacFactory. Another is a data-access layer where an OracleDaoFactory and a MySqlDaoFactory produce matching DAOs.

Q43. What are the benefits of Abstract Factory?

It guarantees that products from one family are used together, isolates concrete classes from clients, makes exchanging product families easy, and promotes consistency across the application.

Q44. What are the drawbacks of Abstract Factory?

Adding a new kind of product forces a change to the abstract factory and all concrete factories. The number of classes and interfaces grows quickly, increasing complexity.

Q45. How does Abstract Factory differ from Factory Method?

Factory Method creates one product through inheritance; Abstract Factory creates a family of products through composition. Abstract Factory is a “factory of factories” made of factory methods.

↑ Contents25

Abstract Factory · continued

Q46. How can an Abstract Factory be implemented using other patterns?

Concrete factories are commonly Singletons (one per family), the creation methods are often Factory Methods, and a factory can also be built from Prototype instances that it clones to produce products.

Q47. Give examples of Abstract Factory in the JDK and frameworks.

DocumentBuilderFactory.newInstance(), TransformerFactory.newInstance(), SAXParserFactory, and in Spring the BeanFactory/ApplicationContext hierarchy. JDBC drivers also behave like abstract factories for Connection, Statement and ResultSet.

Q48. How does Abstract Factory relate to dependency injection?

A DI container can play the role of the abstract factory: profiles or configuration select a family of implementations (for example test versus production beans) and inject them, so application code never calls new on a concrete class.

Builder

INTENT

  • Separate the construction of a complex object from its representation so the same process can create different representations.

PROBLEM AND SOLUTION

  • Constructors with many (optional) parameters are unreadable (the telescoping constructor problem).
  • Build the object step by step, then call build() to obtain the finished product.

IN THE JDK

  • StringBuilder, Stream.Builder, Locale.Builder, HttpRequest.newBuilder(), ProcessBuilder, Lombok @Builder.
Pizza p = new Pizza.Builder("large")
        .cheese(true).pepperoni(true).build();

Q49. What is the Builder pattern and what problem does it solve?

Builder constructs complex objects step by step. It solves the telescoping constructor and JavaBeans-setter problems: it gives readable code, optional parameters, and an object that is fully built (and can be immutable) when build() is called.

↑ Contents28

Builder · continued

Q50. What are the participants in the classic GoF Builder?

Builder (interface with build steps), ConcreteBuilder (implements steps and holds the product), Director (orchestrates the order of steps) and Product (the complex object being built).

Q51. What is the role of the Director? Is it mandatory?

The Director encapsulates a reusable construction sequence (for example “build a vegetarian meal”) and calls the builder steps in order. It is optional; in the fluent builder popularized by Joshua Bloch the client acts as the director.

Q52. What is a fluent builder and why is it popular in Java?

Each setter returns the builder (return this), allowing chained calls. It reads like a DSL, makes optional parameters explicit, lets build() validate invariants, and supports immutable products.

Q53. Give examples of Builder in the JDK and libraries.

StringBuilder.append(), Stream.builder(), Locale.Builder, HttpRequest.newBuilder(), ProcessBuilder, Calendar.Builder, plus Lombok’s @Builder and Guava’s ImmutableList.builder().

↑ Contents29

Builder · continued

Q54. What is the difference between Builder and Factory patterns?

A factory returns a product in one call, usually simple or from a family. A builder assembles a complex object over several steps and gives fine control over its construction. Builder focuses on how an object is put together; Factory on which class is created.

Q55. How does Builder help create immutable objects and enforce validation?

The product has final fields and a private constructor that takes the builder. The builder collects values, and build() validates required fields and cross-field rules before creating the object, so invalid or half-built instances never exist.

Q56. How do you handle Builder with inheritance?

Use a generic self-referencing type (B extends Builder<B>) with an abstract self() method so that chained calls in the parent return the subclass builder type. This is known as the curiously recurring template idiom.

Q57. What are the drawbacks of Builder?

More code (a parallel builder class), a slight runtime cost, and the risk that required fields are forgotten because they are checked only at runtime unless a staged (step) builder is used. It is overkill for objects with two or three parameters.

Prototype

INTENT

  • Specify the kinds of objects to create using a prototypical instance, and create new objects by copying this prototype.

PROBLEM AND SOLUTION

  • Creating an object is expensive (database read, network, heavy computation) or the exact class is known only at runtime.
  • Declare a clone operation; clients copy a configured prototype instead of building from scratch.

IN THE JDK

  • Object.clone() with Cloneable, ArrayList.clone(), HashMap.clone().

Q58. What is the Prototype pattern?

It creates new objects by cloning an existing instance (the prototype) instead of invoking a constructor. It is useful when creation is costly, or when the concrete type is chosen at runtime.

Q59. What is the difference between shallow copy and deep copy?

A shallow copy duplicates the object but shares references to nested objects, so changes leak between copies. A deep copy recursively duplicates nested objects so the copy is fully independent.

↑ Contents32

Prototype · continued

Q60. How does Java support Prototype and what are the problems with clone()?

Through the Cloneable marker interface and Object.clone(). Problems: clone() is protected, Cloneable does not declare the method, the default copy is shallow, it bypasses constructors, interacts badly with final fields and throws a checked CloneNotSupportedException.

Q61. What are alternatives to Object.clone() in Java?

Copy constructors, static copy factory methods, serialization-based deep copy, and mapping libraries or records with a “with” style copy method. Effective Java recommends copy constructors or factories over clone().

Q62. When should you use Prototype?

When classes are determined at runtime, when object initialization is expensive and copies differ only slightly, when you want to avoid parallel factory hierarchies, or when you need to snapshot configured objects (documents, game units, test fixtures).

Q63. What is a prototype registry (manager)?

A key-to-prototype map that holds pre-configured instances. Clients request a clone by key, so they get ready-to-use objects without knowing the concrete class.

↑ Contents33

Prototype · continued

Q64. How does Prototype differ from Factory patterns?

Factories create products by calling constructors in subclasses; Prototype creates them by copying an instance, and needs no creator subclass hierarchy. Prototype can also be used inside an Abstract Factory.

Q65. Give real-world examples of Prototype.

Cloning shapes in a drawing editor, game entities (enemies copied from a template), copying configured documents or test data, Object.clone() in collections, and Spring’s prototype bean scope, which returns a new instance for each request.

Part 3 · Structural Patterns

Adapter

INTENT

  • Convert the interface of a class into another interface clients expect. Adapter lets classes work together that could not otherwise because of incompatible interfaces.

WHEN TO USE

  • Integrating legacy or third-party code, wrapping a library behind your own interface, bridging old and new APIs.

IN THE JDK

  • Arrays.asList(), InputStreamReader (InputStream to Reader), OutputStreamWriter, Collections.enumeration(), Executors.callable().

Q66. What is the Adapter pattern?

Adapter, also called Wrapper, translates calls from the target interface that the client expects into calls on an existing class (the adaptee) with an incompatible interface, without changing either side.

↑ Contents36

Adapter · continued

Q67. What is the difference between a class adapter and an object adapter?

A class adapter extends the adaptee and implements the target; in Java this works only when the target is an interface, it can override adaptee behaviour but is tied to one adaptee class. An object adapter holds the adaptee as a field and delegates to it, which is more flexible and the usual Java choice.

Q68. What are the participants of Adapter?

Target (interface the client uses), Client, Adaptee (existing class with incompatible interface) and Adapter (implements Target and translates requests to the Adaptee).

Q69. When should you use Adapter?

When you want to reuse an existing class whose interface does not match, when integrating third-party libraries or legacy code, or when you want to isolate your code from vendor-specific APIs.

Q70. Give examples of Adapter in the JDK.

InputStreamReader and OutputStreamWriter adapt byte streams to character streams; Arrays.asList() adapts an array to the List interface; Collections.enumeration() adapts a Collection to an Enumeration; Executors.callable() adapts a Runnable to a Callable.

↑ Contents37

Adapter · continued

Q71. How does Adapter differ from Bridge?

Adapter is applied after the design to make incompatible existing classes work together. Bridge is planned up front to decouple an abstraction from its implementation so that both can vary independently.

Q72. How does Adapter differ from Decorator, Proxy and Facade?

Adapter changes the interface. Decorator keeps the interface and adds behaviour. Proxy keeps the interface and controls access. Facade defines a new, simpler interface to a whole subsystem.

Q73. What are the drawbacks of Adapter? What is a two-way adapter?

It adds an extra class and indirection, and heavy translation can hide performance costs. A two-way adapter implements both interfaces so that systems with different expectations can each use it transparently.

Bridge

INTENT

  • Decouple an abstraction from its implementation so that the two can vary independently.

PROBLEM AND SOLUTION

  • Subclassing in two dimensions causes a class explosion (for example Shape × Color, or Remote × Device).
  • Split into two hierarchies: the abstraction holds a reference to an implementor interface.

IN THE JDK

  • JDBC (java.sql.DriverManager + Driver), AWT peers, SLF4J API over logging back-ends.

Q74. What is the Bridge pattern?

Bridge separates a high-level abstraction from its low-level implementation into two independent class hierarchies connected through composition (the “bridge”), so either side can be extended without affecting the other.

Q75. What problem does Bridge solve?

The class explosion caused by combining several dimensions of variation through inheritance. With 3 shapes and 4 colours, inheritance gives 12 classes; Bridge gives 3 + 4 classes composed at runtime.

↑ Contents40

Bridge · continued

Q76. What are the participants of Bridge?

Abstraction (defines the high-level interface and holds an Implementor reference), RefinedAbstraction (extends it), Implementor (interface for implementation classes) and ConcreteImplementor.

Q77. Give a real-world example of Bridge.

A remote control (abstraction: BasicRemote, AdvancedRemote) working with devices (implementor: TV, Radio). Also JDBC: your code uses the API while each database vendor supplies the driver implementation.

Q78. How is Bridge different from Adapter?

Adapter makes unrelated, existing interfaces compatible after the fact. Bridge is a design-time decision that separates abstraction and implementation so they evolve independently.

Q79. How is Bridge different from Strategy?

Both use composition and delegation. Strategy swaps one algorithm inside a context and focuses on behaviour. Bridge structures a whole abstraction/implementation hierarchy and is about separating two dimensions of a class family.

↑ Contents41

Bridge · continued

Q80. What are the benefits and when do you use Bridge?

Use it when both abstraction and implementation need to be extended by subclassing, when implementation choice should be switchable at runtime, or when you want to hide implementation details from clients. Benefits: Open/Closed, SRP and platform independence.

↑ Contents43

Composite

INTENT

  • Compose objects into tree structures to represent part-whole hierarchies. Clients treat individual objects and compositions uniformly.

PROBLEM AND SOLUTION

  • Clients must distinguish leaves from containers.
  • Define a common Component interface; Composite stores children and forwards operations to them.

IN THE JDK

  • java.awt.Container + Component, javax.swing.JComponent, and in frameworks: DOM nodes, JSF UIComponent.

Q81. What is the Composite pattern?

Composite lets you build tree structures where both single objects (leaves) and groups (composites) implement the same interface, so client code can treat them the same way, for example calling size() or render() on a file or a folder.

Q82. What are the participants of Composite?

Component (common interface), Leaf (no children, does the real work), Composite (stores child components and delegates operations to them) and Client (uses the Component interface).

↑ Contents44

Composite · continued

Q83. What is the difference between transparent and safe composites?

In the transparent form, child management methods (add, remove) are declared on Component, giving uniformity but letting leaves throw errors. In the safe form they exist only on Composite, which is type-safe but clients must distinguish leaf and composite.

Q84. Give real-world examples of Composite.

File systems (files and directories), GUI containers and widgets, organization charts, menus and sub-menus, XML/HTML DOM trees, and product bundles in an e-commerce catalog.

Q85. How do you traverse a Composite structure?

By recursion inside the composite operation (each composite calls the same operation on its children), or with an Iterator for flat traversal. Visitor can add new operations over the tree without changing the node classes.

Q86. What are the benefits and drawbacks of Composite?

Benefits: uniform treatment, easy addition of new component types, and simple client code. Drawbacks: the design can become overly general, it is hard to restrict the types of children, and the transparent version sacrifices type safety.

↑ Contents45

Composite · continued

Q87. How does Composite differ from Decorator?

Both use recursive composition. Composite aggregates many children to represent a whole-part tree and sums or forwards results. Decorator wraps exactly one component to add responsibilities and returns the same logical object with added behaviour.

↑ Contents47

Decorator

INTENT

  • Attach additional responsibilities to an object dynamically. Decorators provide a flexible alternative to subclassing for extending functionality.

PROBLEM AND SOLUTION

  • Subclassing for every feature combination produces too many classes.
  • Wrap the object in decorators that implement the same interface and add behaviour before or after delegating.

IN THE JDK

  • java.io streams (BufferedInputStream, DataInputStream), Collections.unmodifiableList(), Collections.synchronizedList(), HttpServletRequestWrapper.
InputStream in = new BufferedInputStream(
                   new GZIPInputStream(
                     new FileInputStream("data.gz")));

Q88. What is the Decorator pattern?

Decorator wraps an object with another object of the same interface that adds new behaviour dynamically, either before or after forwarding the call. Features are combined by stacking decorators at runtime.

↑ Contents48

Decorator · continued

Q89. What are the participants of Decorator?

Component (interface), ConcreteComponent (the base object), Decorator (abstract wrapper that holds a Component reference) and ConcreteDecorator (adds specific behaviour).

Q90. Give the classic example of Decorator in Java.

The java.io package: BufferedReader wraps a Reader, GZIPInputStream wraps an InputStream, DataOutputStream wraps an OutputStream. Each adds one capability (buffering, compression, typed data) while keeping the same base interface.

Q91. Why use Decorator instead of inheritance?

Inheritance is static and applies to the whole class, causing a class explosion for feature combinations. Decorator adds or removes behaviour per object at runtime, and combinations are made by composing decorators in any order.

Q92. How does Decorator differ from Proxy?

Both wrap an object with the same interface. Decorator adds responsibilities and the client usually builds the wrapper chain. Proxy controls access (lazy load, security, remote) and often manages the lifecycle of the real object itself.

Q93. What are the benefits of Decorator?

Flexible runtime extension, adherence to Open/Closed and SRP (each decorator has one concern), avoids feature-laden base classes, and allows arbitrary combinations.

↑ Contents49

Decorator · continued

Q94. What are the drawbacks of Decorator?

Many small look-alike classes, wrapper order can matter, identity checks fail (the decorated object is not the original), and debugging deep wrapper chains is harder.

Q95. Give other examples of Decorator and compare it to Chain of Responsibility.

Collections.unmodifiableList(), synchronizedMap() and Servlet request/response wrappers. In a Decorator every layer typically contributes to the result and calls the next, while in Chain of Responsibility any handler may stop the request.

↑ Contents50
↑ Contents51

Facade

INTENT

  • Provide a unified, simpler interface to a set of interfaces in a subsystem. Facade defines a higher-level interface that makes the subsystem easier to use.

PROBLEM AND SOLUTION

  • Clients need to coordinate many classes to do one common task.
  • A facade class exposes simple methods and hides the orchestration.

IN THE JDK AND FRAMEWORKS

  • javax.faces.context.FacesContext, java.net.URL.openStream(), SLF4J LoggerFactory, Spring’s JdbcTemplate, service layers in n-tier applications.

Q96. What is the Facade pattern?

Facade offers a single simplified entry point to a complex subsystem. The facade delegates to subsystem classes, so clients depend only on the facade and not on the many internal classes.

Q97. What problem does Facade solve and what are its participants?

It reduces the coupling and learning curve of complex subsystems. Participants: Facade (knows which subsystem classes handle a request and delegates), Subsystem classes (do the work, unaware of the facade) and Client.

↑ Contents52

Facade · continued

Q98. Give real-world examples of Facade.

A home theater “watchMovie()” method that turns on the projector, amplifier and player; an order-processing service that calls inventory, payment and shipping; a JdbcTemplate; a travel-booking service that wraps flight, hotel and car APIs.

Q99. How does Facade differ from Adapter?

Facade simplifies a whole subsystem by creating a new, easier interface. Adapter makes one existing interface fit another expected interface. Facade is about simplification; Adapter is about compatibility.

Q100. How does Facade differ from Mediator?

Facade is a one-way convenience: clients call the facade, and the subsystem does not know about it. Mediator is two-way: colleague objects know the mediator and communicate through it, so interaction logic is centralized.

Q101. Does a Facade prevent direct access to the subsystem?

No. Facade only provides a convenient path; advanced clients can still use the subsystem classes directly. Strict encapsulation is achieved by visibility rules (package-private, modules), not by the pattern itself.

↑ Contents53

Facade · continued

Q102. What are the benefits and risks of Facade?

Benefits: simpler client code, looser coupling, layered architecture, easier subsystem refactoring. Risk: the facade can grow into a God Object that does too much, so keep it thin and split by use case.

↑ Contents55

Flyweight

INTENT

  • Use sharing to support large numbers of fine-grained objects efficiently.

PROBLEM AND SOLUTION

  • Millions of similar objects exhaust memory.
  • Separate intrinsic state (shared, immutable) from extrinsic state (supplied by the client); keep shared objects in a factory pool.

IN THE JDK

  • Integer.valueOf() cache (-128 to 127), Boolean.valueOf(), Character.valueOf(), String intern pool, Byte and Short caches.

Q103. What is the Flyweight pattern?

Flyweight reduces memory use by sharing common state between many objects. A factory returns existing instances for identical intrinsic state, and clients pass the varying state in at call time.

Q104. What are intrinsic and extrinsic state?

Intrinsic state is context-independent, stored inside the flyweight and shared (for example a character’s glyph and font). Extrinsic state depends on context and is supplied by the client (for example the character’s position on the page).

↑ Contents56

Flyweight · continued

Q105. What are the participants of Flyweight?

Flyweight (interface), ConcreteFlyweight (stores intrinsic state, must be shareable), FlyweightFactory (creates and pools flyweights) and Client (computes or stores extrinsic state).

Q106. Give examples of Flyweight in the JDK.

Integer.valueOf() returns cached objects for -128 to 127, as do Byte, Short, Long and Character caches; Boolean.TRUE and FALSE are shared; the String constant pool and String.intern() share identical strings.

Q107. When should you use Flyweight?

When the application uses a huge number of objects, storage cost is high because of that count, most state can be made extrinsic, and object identity does not matter. Examples: text editors, particle systems, map tiles, game forests.

Q108. How does Flyweight differ from Singleton and an Object Pool?

Singleton keeps exactly one instance of a class. Flyweight keeps one shared instance per distinct intrinsic state. An object pool reuses mutable, expensive objects that are checked out and returned, while flyweights are immutable and shared simultaneously.

↑ Contents57

Flyweight · continued

Q109. What are the drawbacks of Flyweight?

Added complexity in separating state, CPU cost to compute or pass extrinsic state, and the need for flyweights to be immutable and thread-safe. It only pays off when there are many duplicates.

↑ Contents59

Proxy

INTENT

  • Provide a surrogate or placeholder for another object to control access to it.

TYPES OF PROXY

  • Virtual (lazy creation), Remote (local stand-in for a remote object), Protection (access control), Smart reference (counting, locking), Caching.

IN THE JDK AND FRAMEWORKS

  • java.lang.reflect.Proxy, java.rmi stubs, Spring AOP (@Transactional, @Cacheable), Hibernate lazy-loading proxies.
Service p = (Service) Proxy.newProxyInstance(
    loader, new Class[]{Service.class},
    (proxy, method, args) -> { log(method); return method.invoke(real, args); });

Q110. What is the Proxy pattern?

Proxy provides an object with the same interface as the real subject that controls access to it. The proxy can add lazy initialization, access checks, caching or logging before or after delegating to the real object.

↑ Contents60

Proxy · continued

Q111. What are the types of proxies?

Virtual proxy (defers expensive creation), remote proxy (represents an object in another address space), protection proxy (checks permissions), smart reference (reference counting, locking), and caching proxy (stores results).

Q112. What are the participants of Proxy?

Subject (common interface), RealSubject (the actual object) and Proxy (holds a reference to RealSubject, implements Subject and controls access).

Q113. What is the difference between a static proxy and a dynamic proxy in Java?

A static proxy is a hand-written class per interface, created at compile time. A dynamic proxy is generated at runtime, using java.lang.reflect.Proxy (interfaces only) with an InvocationHandler, or bytecode libraries such as CGLIB and ByteBuddy (classes).

Q114. Where is Proxy used in Spring and Hibernate?

Spring AOP wraps beans in JDK or CGLIB proxies to apply @Transactional, @Async, @Cacheable and security advice. Hibernate returns proxy entities for lazy associations and loads data on first access.

↑ Contents61

Proxy · continued

Q115. How does Proxy differ from Decorator?

Structure is similar. Proxy controls access to the object and often creates or manages the real subject. Decorator adds new functionality and is composed by the client. The difference is intent, not structure.

Q116. How does Proxy differ from Adapter and Facade?

Proxy keeps the same interface as the subject. Adapter converts to a different interface. Facade presents a simpler interface to several objects.

Q117. What are the drawbacks of Proxy?

Extra indirection and latency, possible surprises with self-invocation (a call inside the same class bypasses the Spring proxy), final classes or methods that cannot be proxied by CGLIB, and more classes to maintain in the static form.

Part 4 · Behavioral Patterns

Chain of Responsibility

INTENT

  • Avoid coupling the sender of a request to its receiver by giving more than one object a chance to handle it. Chain the receivers and pass the request along until one handles it.

IN THE JDK AND FRAMEWORKS

  • javax.servlet.Filter / FilterChain, Spring Security filter chain, java.util.logging Logger parent handlers, catch blocks for exceptions, Netty ChannelPipeline.

Q118. What is the Chain of Responsibility pattern?

Handlers are linked in a chain. A request enters the first handler, which either processes it or forwards it to the next. The sender does not know which handler will process the request.

Q119. What are the participants of Chain of Responsibility?

Handler (interface with handle() and a link to the successor), ConcreteHandler (handles requests it is responsible for or forwards the rest) and Client (initiates the request to the head of the chain).

↑ Contents64

Chain of Responsibility · continued

Q120. Give real-world examples of Chain of Responsibility.

Servlet filters and the Spring Security filter chain, logging levels, exception handling through catch blocks, approval workflows (manager, director, VP), middleware pipelines, and support escalation levels.

Q121. What is the difference between a pure and an impure chain?

In a pure chain exactly one handler processes the request and others only forward it. In an impure (common) chain several handlers may act on the request, as with servlet filters, and any handler may stop the flow.

Q122. How does Chain of Responsibility differ from Decorator?

Decorator layers add behaviour and always delegate to the wrapped object. In a chain, a handler can decide to stop the request and not call the next handler, and handlers can be independent processors.

Q123. What are the benefits and drawbacks of Chain of Responsibility?

Benefits: reduced coupling, flexible assignment of responsibilities, easy to add or reorder handlers. Drawbacks: no guarantee that a request is handled, debugging is harder, and long chains can hurt performance.

↑ Contents65

Chain of Responsibility · continued

Q124. How can you implement the chain in modern Java?

Link handler objects with a next reference, use a List of handlers and iterate, or compose functions (Function.andThen, Predicate.and) with lambdas. Frameworks such as servlets pass a FilterChain object that each filter calls.

↑ Contents67

Command

INTENT

  • Encapsulate a request as an object, thereby letting you parameterize clients with different requests, queue or log requests, and support undoable operations.

IN THE JDK AND FRAMEWORKS

  • Runnable, Callable, javax.swing.Action, java.util.concurrent task queues, Spring Batch steps, CQRS command objects.

Q125. What is the Command pattern?

Command turns a request into a stand-alone object containing everything needed to perform it (receiver, method and arguments). This decouples the object that invokes the operation from the one that knows how to perform it.

Q126. What are the participants of Command?

Command (interface with execute()), ConcreteCommand (binds a Receiver to an action), Receiver (performs the work), Invoker (asks the command to run) and Client (creates the command and sets its receiver).

Q127. How do you implement undo and redo with Command?

Add an undo() method to the command and store executed commands in a history stack. Undo pops and calls undo(); redo re-executes from a second stack. Commands may store a Memento or the previous state to restore it.

↑ Contents68

Command · continued

Q128. Give examples of Command in Java.

Runnable and Callable submitted to an ExecutorService, Swing Actions behind buttons and menus, transactional job queues, macro recorders, and command objects in CQRS architectures.

Q129. How does Command differ from Strategy?

Command encapsulates a request (what and on whom) and is often executed once, queued or undone. Strategy encapsulates an interchangeable algorithm that a context uses repeatedly to do the same job in different ways.

Q130. What is a macro command?

A composite command that holds a list of commands and executes them in sequence. It uses Composite to treat a group of commands like a single command, supporting batch operations and scripted workflows.

Q131. What are the benefits and drawbacks of Command?

Benefits: decoupling, undo/redo, queuing, logging, scheduling, and easy extension. Drawbacks: one class per action, which increases the class count (reduced in modern Java with lambdas).

↑ Contents69

Interpreter

INTENT

  • Given a language, define a representation for its grammar along with an interpreter that uses the representation to interpret sentences in the language.

IN THE JDK AND FRAMEWORKS

  • java.util.regex.Pattern, java.text.Format and subclasses, Spring Expression Language (SpEL), javax.el.ELResolver, SQL and rule engines.

Q132. What is the Interpreter pattern?

It represents each grammar rule of a simple language as a class and builds an abstract syntax tree of these objects. Calling interpret(context) on the root evaluates the whole sentence.

Q133. What are the participants of Interpreter?

AbstractExpression (interpret method), TerminalExpression (literals and variables), NonterminalExpression (rules composed of other expressions), Context (global information such as variable values) and Client (builds the syntax tree).

Q134. Give examples of Interpreter.

Regular expressions (java.util.regex), mathematical expression evaluators, Spring SpEL, boolean rule engines, SQL parsers and MessageFormat. A typical interview example is evaluating “5 + 3 − 2”.

↑ Contents70

Interpreter · continued

Q135. When should you use Interpreter and when should you avoid it?

Use it for simple grammars where efficiency is not critical. Avoid it for complex grammars because the class count explodes and performance is poor; use parser generators such as ANTLR instead.

Q136. How does Interpreter relate to Composite and Visitor?

The abstract syntax tree is a Composite (terminal and non-terminal nodes). Visitor can add operations to the tree (printing, type-checking) without changing the expression classes, and Iterator can traverse it.

Iterator

INTENT

  • Provide a way to access the elements of an aggregate object sequentially without exposing its underlying representation.

IN THE JDK

  • java.util.Iterator, Iterable (enhanced for-loop), ListIterator, Enumeration, Spliterator, Scanner, ResultSet.

Q137. What is the Iterator pattern?

An iterator object encapsulates traversal of a collection with methods such as hasNext() and next(). Clients traverse elements without knowing whether the collection is an array, list, tree or graph.

Q138. What are the participants of Iterator?

Iterator (interface), ConcreteIterator (tracks the traversal position), Aggregate or Iterable (declares a method that creates the iterator) and ConcreteAggregate (returns its own iterator).

Q139. How is Iterator implemented in Java?

Collections implement Iterable<T> with iterator(), which returns an Iterator<T> (hasNext, next, remove). The enhanced for-loop uses it. ListIterator adds bidirectional traversal and modification; Spliterator supports parallel streams.

↑ Contents72

Iterator · continued

Q140. What is the difference between external and internal iteration?

External iteration means the client controls the loop (Iterator, for-each). Internal iteration means the collection controls it and you pass behaviour, as with forEach() and the Streams API, which allows optimization and parallelism.

Q141. What is the difference between fail-fast and fail-safe iterators?

Fail-fast iterators (ArrayList, HashMap) throw ConcurrentModificationException when the collection is structurally modified during iteration. Fail-safe or weakly consistent iterators (CopyOnWriteArrayList, ConcurrentHashMap) iterate over a snapshot or tolerate changes without throwing.

Q142. What are the benefits of Iterator?

It hides internal structure, supports several simultaneous traversals, provides a uniform interface across collection types and lets you introduce new traversal strategies without changing the collection.

Mediator

INTENT

  • Define an object that encapsulates how a set of objects interact. Mediator promotes loose coupling by keeping objects from referring to each other explicitly.

IN THE JDK AND FRAMEWORKS

  • java.util.concurrent.ExecutorService, java.util.Timer, Spring MVC DispatcherServlet, MVC controllers.

Q143. What is the Mediator pattern?

Colleagues do not talk to each other directly; they send messages to a mediator that routes them. This replaces many-to-many relationships with many-to-one-to-many, reducing coupling.

Q144. What are the participants of Mediator?

Mediator (interface), ConcreteMediator (coordinates colleagues), Colleague (each knows only the mediator) and ConcreteColleague (sends and receives through the mediator).

Q145. Give real-world examples of Mediator.

An air-traffic control tower, a chat room server, a GUI dialog that coordinates widgets, Spring’s DispatcherServlet, a message broker and the controller in MVC.

↑ Contents74

Mediator · continued

Q146. How does Mediator differ from Observer?

Observer defines a one-to-many, usually one-way subscription so that subjects broadcast changes. Mediator centralizes two-way communication and interaction rules between peers. A mediator can use Observer internally to receive colleague events.

Q147. How does Mediator differ from Facade?

Facade simplifies access to a subsystem in one direction and the subsystem is unaware of it. Mediator coordinates mutual communication among colleagues who are aware of it.

Q148. What are the drawbacks of Mediator?

The mediator can become a God Object holding all interaction logic, creating a single point of complexity and failure. Split it into several focused mediators if it grows.

Memento

INTENT

  • Without violating encapsulation, capture and externalize an object’s internal state so that the object can be restored to this state later.

IN THE JDK AND FRAMEWORKS

  • java.io.Serializable snapshots, text-editor undo stacks, game save points, transaction savepoints.

Q149. What is the Memento pattern?

Memento stores a snapshot of an object’s state in a separate object, so the object (originator) can be rolled back later, without exposing its internals to other classes.

Q150. What are the roles in Memento?

Originator (creates a memento of its state and restores from it), Memento (immutable state holder) and Caretaker (keeps mementos, for example a history stack, but never inspects or modifies them).

Q151. How does Memento preserve encapsulation?

Mementos have a narrow interface for caretakers (opaque, no access to state) and a wide interface for the originator. In Java this is done with a private or package-private nested class inside the originator.

↑ Contents76

Memento · continued

Q152. How do Memento and Command work together for undo?

The command performs the action and stores a memento of the state before execution; undo() restores that memento. This is useful when it is easier to restore a snapshot than to reverse the operation.

Q153. Give real-world examples of Memento.

Undo in text and graphics editors, saved games, database transaction savepoints and rollbacks, browser form state, and serialization-based checkpoints.

Q154. What are the drawbacks of Memento and how can they be reduced?

Snapshots of large objects consume memory and copying is costly, and caretakers cannot know the memento’s size. Mitigate by storing only incremental changes (deltas), limiting history size or using copy-on-write.

Observer

INTENT

  • Define a one-to-many dependency between objects so that when one object changes state, all its dependents are notified and updated automatically.

IN THE JDK AND FRAMEWORKS

  • java.util.EventListener family, PropertyChangeListener, Swing listeners, java.util.concurrent.Flow, Spring ApplicationEvent, RxJava / Reactor.

Q155. What is the Observer pattern?

A Subject maintains a list of Observers and notifies them automatically of state changes by calling their update method. Observers can be added or removed at runtime, and the subject need not know their concrete types.

Q156. What are the participants of Observer?

Subject (attach, detach, notify), ConcreteSubject (stores state, notifies on change), Observer (interface with update()) and ConcreteObserver (reacts to the notification).

Q157. What is the difference between the push and pull models?

In push, the subject sends the changed data in the notification. In pull, the subject only signals a change and observers query the subject for what they need. Push is simpler but couples observers to data shape; pull is more flexible.

↑ Contents78

Observer · continued

Q158. Which JDK classes implement Observer?

java.util.Observer and Observable (deprecated), the EventListener-based model in AWT and Swing, java.beans.PropertyChangeListener, and java.util.concurrent.Flow (Publisher/Subscriber) for reactive streams.

Q159. Why were java.util.Observable and Observer deprecated?

Since Java 9 they are deprecated because Observable is a class (forcing inheritance), its notification is not type-safe, it does not support rich event models, and its thread-safety guarantees are weak. Use PropertyChangeSupport, the Flow API or custom listener interfaces.

Q160. How does Observer differ from Publish-Subscribe?

In Observer the subject knows its observers directly and notification is typically synchronous in-process. In Pub/Sub publishers and subscribers are decoupled through a broker or event channel, with topics and often asynchronous delivery.

Q161. What is the lapsed listener problem?

Observers that are registered but never deregistered stay strongly referenced by the subject and cannot be garbage collected, causing memory leaks. Always unregister, or use weak references.

↑ Contents79

Observer · continued

Q162. Where is Observer used in Spring and reactive programming?

Spring’s ApplicationEventPublisher and @EventListener implement it. In MVC architecture, the view observes the model. Reactive libraries (RxJava, Reactor) generalize it with back-pressure and operators.

State

INTENT

  • Allow an object to alter its behaviour when its internal state changes. The object will appear to change its class.

PROBLEM AND SOLUTION

  • Large if/switch statements on a status field repeated in many methods.
  • Model each state as a class implementing a State interface; the Context delegates to the current state object.

Q163. What is the State pattern?

State encapsulates state-specific behaviour into separate classes. The context holds a reference to the current state object and delegates requests to it; state objects can change the context’s state, so behaviour changes at runtime.

Q164. What are the participants of State?

Context (holds the current State and exposes the client interface), State (interface for state-specific behaviour) and ConcreteState classes (implement behaviour for one state and may trigger transitions).

Q165. Give real-world examples of State.

A vending machine (idle, has-money, dispensing), a TCP connection (listen, established, closed), order lifecycle (new, paid, shipped, delivered), document workflow (draft, review, published) and media player controls.

↑ Contents82

State · continued

Q166. How does State differ from Strategy?

Structure is identical. Strategy is chosen by the client and strategies usually do not know each other. State transitions are driven by the context or by the states themselves, and the states often know the next states. Strategy is about algorithms; State is about lifecycle.

Q167. Who should define the state transitions: the context or the states?

If the transition rules are fixed and simple, the context can hold them centrally. Letting states decide the next state is more flexible and decentralizes the logic but couples the state classes to each other.

Q168. What are the benefits of State?

It removes large conditional blocks, localizes state-specific behaviour (SRP), makes new states easy to add (OCP) and makes transitions explicit.

Q169. What are the drawbacks of State, and how can state objects be shared?

More classes for few states, and risk of overkill. Stateless state objects can be shared as Singletons or Flyweights. For simple cases use an enum with abstract methods or a state-machine library such as Spring Statemachine.

Strategy

INTENT

  • Define a family of algorithms, encapsulate each one, and make them interchangeable. Strategy lets the algorithm vary independently from the clients that use it.

IN THE JDK

  • java.util.Comparator (Collections.sort), java.util.zip.Checksum, javax.servlet.http.HttpServlet response compression, ThreadPoolExecutor RejectedExecutionHandler, java.security.MessageDigest.
list.sort(Comparator.comparing(Employee::getSalary));  // strategy as lambda

Q170. What is the Strategy pattern?

Strategy puts each algorithm in its own class behind a common interface, and the context holds a reference to one strategy that it can switch at runtime, replacing conditional logic with polymorphism.

Q171. What are the participants of Strategy?

Strategy (interface), ConcreteStrategy (an algorithm implementation), Context (configured with a strategy and calls it) and Client (selects the strategy).

↑ Contents84

Strategy · continued

Q172. Give examples of Strategy in Java.

Comparator passed to sort(), RejectedExecutionHandler in a thread pool, payment methods (card, UPI, wallet), compression algorithms, pricing or discount rules, and routing algorithms.

Q173. How do lambdas and functional interfaces affect Strategy?

When a strategy has one method, it can be a functional interface and each strategy a lambda or method reference, eliminating the need for separate classes. Comparator, Predicate and Function are all strategies in this form.

Q174. What is the difference between Strategy, State and Template Method?

Strategy swaps the whole algorithm through composition and the client selects it. State swaps behaviour based on internal state and transitions automatically. Template Method fixes the algorithm skeleton in a base class and lets subclasses override steps through inheritance.

Q175. How do you select a Strategy at runtime without if/else?

Use a Map from key to strategy (populated from enum values or dependency injection), or a factory that returns the right strategy, so adding a strategy does not change the selection code.

↑ Contents85

Strategy · continued

Q176. What are the benefits of Strategy?

Interchangeable algorithms, elimination of conditionals, Open/Closed compliance, isolation of algorithm details from the context, and easier unit testing of each algorithm.

Q177. What are the drawbacks of Strategy?

Clients must know the differences between strategies to choose one, there are more objects, and for just a couple of rarely changing algorithms a simple conditional may be clearer.

↑ Contents87

Template Method

INTENT

  • Define the skeleton of an algorithm in an operation, deferring some steps to subclasses. Template Method lets subclasses redefine certain steps without changing the algorithm’s structure.

IN THE JDK AND FRAMEWORKS

  • java.io.InputStream.read(byte[]), AbstractList / AbstractMap, HttpServlet.service() with doGet()/doPost(), Spring JdbcTemplate and TransactionTemplate (callbacks), JUnit setUp/tearDown.

Q178. What is the Template Method pattern?

An abstract class defines a final method that calls a fixed sequence of steps; some steps are abstract or optional hooks that subclasses implement. The base class controls the order, and subclasses supply the variations.

Q179. What are the participants and what is a hook method?

AbstractClass (template method plus primitive operations) and ConcreteClass (implements the primitives). A hook is an optional step with an empty or default implementation that subclasses may override.

↑ Contents88

Template Method · continued

Q180. What is the Hollywood Principle?

“Don’t call us, we’ll call you.” The framework (high-level class) calls the application’s (low-level) methods, not the other way round. Template Method is a classic example of this inversion of control.

Q181. Give examples of Template Method in the JDK and frameworks.

HttpServlet.service() calls doGet() or doPost(); AbstractList builds many methods on get() and size(); InputStream.read(byte[]) is built on read(); JdbcTemplate manages connection handling and calls your callback; JUnit calls setUp, test and tearDown.

Q182. Why should the template method be final?

To prevent subclasses from changing the algorithm’s order and thereby breaking its invariants. Only the designated steps should be overridable.

Q183. How does Template Method differ from Strategy?

Template Method uses inheritance and varies parts of an algorithm at compile time. Strategy uses composition and replaces the whole algorithm at runtime. Prefer Strategy for flexibility.

↑ Contents89

Template Method · continued

Q184. What are the benefits and drawbacks of Template Method?

Benefits: removes duplicate code, clear extension points and controlled customization. Drawbacks: reliance on inheritance, rigid skeleton, possible Liskov violations if subclasses break the expected step order, and many subclasses for many variations.

Visitor

INTENT

  • Represent an operation to be performed on the elements of an object structure. Visitor lets you define a new operation without changing the classes of the elements on which it operates.

IN THE JDK AND TOOLS

  • java.nio.file.FileVisitor / SimpleFileVisitor, javax.lang.model.element.ElementVisitor, ASM ClassVisitor, ANTLR parse tree visitors, Java compiler tree API.

Q185. What is the Visitor pattern?

Visitor moves operations out of the element classes into a separate visitor class. Each element has an accept(visitor) method that calls the visitor’s method for its own type, so new operations are added by writing new visitors.

Q186. What are the participants of Visitor?

Visitor (declares a visit method per element type), ConcreteVisitor (implements an operation), Element (declares accept), ConcreteElement (implements accept by calling visitor.visit(this)) and ObjectStructure (collection of elements that can be enumerated).

↑ Contents92

Visitor · continued

Q187. What is double dispatch and why does Visitor need it?

Java chooses a method by the runtime type of the receiver only (single dispatch). Visitor calls element.accept(visitor), which then calls visitor.visit(this); the operation is selected by both the element type and the visitor type, achieving double dispatch.

Q188. When should you use Visitor?

When the object structure is stable but you often add new operations, when you need many unrelated operations on a hierarchy (export, validate, price), or when you want to gather related behaviour in one class instead of scattering it across elements.

Q189. What are the drawbacks of Visitor?

Adding a new element class requires changing the Visitor interface and every visitor, which violates the Open/Closed Principle for elements. It can also break encapsulation because elements must expose state to visitors.

Q190. Give examples of Visitor in Java.

Files.walkFileTree() with FileVisitor, the annotation-processing ElementVisitor, compiler ASTs and bytecode tools like ASM. In modern Java (21+), sealed interfaces with pattern-matching switch often replace Visitor.

↑ Contents93

Visitor · continued

Q191. How does Visitor work with Composite and Interpreter?

Composite trees are a natural object structure: accept() on a composite visits itself and passes the visitor to its children. In Interpreter, visitors evaluate, print or optimize the abstract syntax tree without changing node classes.

Part 5 · Comparisons and Scenarios

Comparisons and Scenario Questions

ONE-LINE DIFFERENTIATORS

  • Adapter: change an interface. Decorator: add behaviour. Proxy: control access. Facade: simplify a subsystem.
  • Strategy: swap algorithm (client chooses). State: swap behaviour (lifecycle chooses). Template Method: vary steps by inheritance.
  • Factory Method: one product via subclass. Abstract Factory: product families. Builder: stepwise construction. Prototype: copy.
  • Observer: one-to-many notification. Mediator: many-to-many coordination.

Q192. Which pattern would you use to replace a large if-else or switch on a type?

Polymorphism using Strategy (if choosing an algorithm), State (if behaviour depends on lifecycle), or Factory Method (if choosing which object to create). Combine with a map of key to implementation to avoid conditional lookup.

Q193. Which patterns would you use to design a logging framework?

Singleton or a LoggerFactory (Factory Method) to obtain loggers, Strategy for appenders and formatters, Chain of Responsibility for log-level handling, Observer to notify appenders, Decorator for async or buffered appenders, and Builder for configuration.

↑ Contents96

Comparisons and Scenario Questions · continued

Q194. Which patterns would you use for a notification system (email, SMS, push)?

Strategy or Abstract Factory for channels, Factory Method to create the right notification, Observer for event subscription, Decorator for adding retry or logging, Template Method for the send flow and Chain of Responsibility for fallback channels.

Q195. Which patterns are involved in the Java I/O library?

Decorator (BufferedReader, GZIPInputStream), Adapter (InputStreamReader), Template Method (InputStream.read(byte[]) built on read()), Factory Method (Files.newInputStream) and Iterator-like streaming (Files.lines).

Q196. How would you implement undo/redo in an application?

Command to encapsulate actions with undo(), Memento to store prior state when reversal is hard, Composite for macro commands, and two stacks (undo and redo) as the caretaker.

Q197. Which patterns would you apply in an e-commerce checkout?

Strategy (payment and discount), Factory (payment gateways), Builder (order), Facade (checkout service over inventory, payment, shipping), State (order lifecycle), Observer (notifications and events), Chain of Responsibility (validation and fraud checks) and Decorator (price add-ons).

Comparisons and Scenario Questions · continued

Q198. Are GoF patterns still relevant in modern Java with lambdas, records, sealed types and DI?

Yes, as vocabulary and design thinking, but implementations have changed. Lambdas make Strategy, Command and Template Method lightweight, enums cover Singleton, records and sealed types simplify Builder-style data and Visitor, and DI containers handle Factory and Singleton scope. The intent matters more than the class diagram.

Q199. What are common mistakes when applying design patterns?

Forcing a pattern where none is needed, mixing up similar patterns (State vs Strategy, Proxy vs Decorator), over-abstracting, ignoring the trade-offs, and memorizing UML instead of understanding intent.

Q200. How should you answer “Which design patterns have you used in your project?”

Pick three or four real examples, state the problem, the pattern and the result. For example: Strategy for payment-provider selection, Builder for complex request objects, Observer/event listeners for domain events, and Proxy through Spring AOP for transactions and caching.
